⚡ Understanding Economic Calendar Impact Levels

🔴 High Impact
Major Market Movers

Events that typically cause price movements of 50+ pips within 15 minutes. These are the most significant economic releases that professional traders watch closely.

Examples:
  • 🇺🇸 Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)
  • 🏦 Federal Reserve Interest Rate Decisions
  • 📊 GDP Growth Rate Releases
  • 💰 Central Bank Policy Statements
  • 📈 Consumer Price Index (CPI)
⚠️ Trading Tip Avoid trading 15 minutes before major releases. Spreads widen significantly and slippage is common. Wait for the initial volatility spike to pass.
🟡 Medium Impact
Regional Indicators

Events causing moderate volatility of 20-50 pips. These releases affect specific currency pairs or regional markets but may not move all majors.

Examples:
  • 🏭 Manufacturing PMI
  • 🏠 Housing Starts & Sales
  • 👔 Unemployment Claims
  • 🛒 Retail Sales
  • 🏦 ECB/BOE Minutes
💡 Strategy Good for swing traders. These events create opportunities but without the extreme volatility of high-impact releases. Perfect for practicing news trading strategies.
🟢 Low Impact
Minor Data Releases

Smaller releases with minimal immediate impact (less than 20 pips). These provide context and sentiment but rarely cause significant price movements alone.

Examples:
  • 📢 Speeches by Minor Officials
  • 📊 Consumer Confidence (Regional)
  • 🏦 Bank Holiday Announcements
  • 📋 Building Permits
  • 🗳️ Minor Political Events
📚 Use Case Track these for overall market sentiment and longer-term fundamental analysis. Don't expect immediate trading opportunities from these releases.

📖 How to Read Economic Calendar Like a Pro

Actual vs Forecast

The "Actual" is the real data released. The "Forecast" is what economists predicted. When Actual beats Forecast, it's generally bullish for that currency.

Real Example:

US NFP Actual: 250K vs Forecast: 180K
Result: USD strengthened by 85 pips vs EUR in 30 minutes.
Why? Better employment = stronger economy = higher interest rate expectations.

Previous Data

The "Previous" shows last period's value. Use this to identify trends. Are numbers improving or deteriorating? This gives context beyond single releases.

Real Example:

GDP Growth: Previous 2.5% → Actual 3.2%
This shows acceleration in growth, very bullish signal.
Even if it matched forecast, the upward trend matters!

Revisions Matter

Pay attention to revisions of previous data. If last month's NFP is revised upward, that's additional bullish news even if current release disappoints.

Real Example:

NFP Actual: 160K (below 180K forecast) = Bearish
BUT Previous revised from 200K to 250K = Bullish revision
Net effect: Market often reacts positively to strong revisions!

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What does "Actual" vs "Forecast" mean?
Forecast: The market's expectation before the data release (consensus from economists).

Actual: The real number released by government/central bank.

Market Impact: When Actual > Forecast, the currency typically strengthens because the economy performed better than expected. When Actual < Forecast, the currency usually weakens.
Should I trade immediately when news releases?
No! This is a common mistake. During major news releases:

• Spreads widen dramatically (EUR/USD can go from 1 pip to 10+ pips)
• Slippage is common (your order fills at worse price)
• Initial spike often reverses within 5-15 minutes

Recommended: Wait 15 minutes after release, watch for confirmation on 15M chart, then enter with tight stop-loss.
How accurate is the economic calendar data?
Our calendar uses TradingView data, which aggregates from official sources (government statistical offices, central banks). Accuracy is typically 99%+ for scheduled releases.

However, note that:
• Release times can occasionally change
• Data is subject to revisions
• Forecasts are estimates from economists, not guarantees

Always cross-check important events with official sources like Fed.gov, ECB.europa.eu, etc.
Why did the market move opposite to the news?
This happens frequently! Reasons include:

1. "Buy the rumor, sell the fact": Market already priced in good news before release
2. Forward guidance matters more: Central bank hints at future policy
3. Revisions overshadow current data: Upward revision of previous month can matter more
4. Conflicting indicators: Good employment but bad wage growth = mixed signal
5. Risk sentiment: Overall market mood can override single data points

This is why fundamental analysis is complex - never trade on single data point alone!
Which economic events are most important for Forex?
Top 5 Market Movers:

1. Central Bank Interest Rate Decisions (Fed, ECB, BOE, BOJ)
2. Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP) - US employment report
3. Consumer Price Index (CPI) - Inflation data
4. GDP Growth Rate - Economic growth
5. Central Bank Press Conferences - Forward guidance

These typically cause 50-200 pip movements and create best trading opportunities for Forex traders.

Should beginners trade economic news?
Recommended for beginners: Practice in Demo first!

News trading is high risk, high reward. Before trading real money:

1. Open a free demo account with $10,000 virtual funds
2. Practice trading 10-20 major news releases in demo
3. Learn to read market reaction (fake-out vs real breakout)
4. Master tight stop-loss placement
5. Only go live when you're consistently profitable in demo

Many professional traders avoid news trading entirely - it's optional, not required for success!
How do I combine technical and fundamental analysis?
Best Practice: Use Both Together

Fundamental (Calendar): Tells you why market might move and when
Technical (Charts): Tells you where to enter and where to exit

Example Strategy:
1. Calendar shows NFP at 8:30 AM (Fundamental timing)
2. Data beats expectations (Fundamental direction = USD up)
3. Wait for EUR/USD to break support level on 15M chart (Technical confirmation)
4. Enter short with stop above recent high (Technical risk management)
