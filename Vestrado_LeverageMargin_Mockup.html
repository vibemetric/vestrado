How Leverage & Margin Work

Choose your experience level to see relevant information and examples

📌 What is Leverage?

Leverage is borrowed money from your broker that allows you to control a larger position than your actual capital. Think of it like a magnifying glass for your trades.

💡
Simple Example
Your Capital: $1,000
Leverage: 1:100
Position You Control: $100,000
If Market Moves 1%: +$1,000 (100% gain) OR -$1,000 (100% loss)

💰 What is Margin?

Margin is the amount of money you need to keep in your account to open and maintain a leveraged position. It's like a security deposit.

📐
Calculation Formula
Position Size: $10,000
Leverage: 1:50
Required Margin = Position ÷ Leverage
You Need: $200 in your account

⚠️ Why Higher Leverage = Higher Risk

Higher leverage means smaller price movements can wipe out your account. Here's a comparison:

✓
1:10 Leverage (Safer)

$1,000 account
Controls: $10,000
To lose 100%: Market must move -10%

⚠️
1:500 Leverage (Risky)

$1,000 account
Controls: $500,000
To lose 100%: Market must move -0.2%

🎯 Dynamic Leverage & Position Sizing

Leverage is not fixed - it changes based on your position size (notional value). Larger positions = lower maximum leverage for risk management.

📊
Tiered Leverage Example
$0 - $50K notional: Max 1:500
$50K - $200K: Max 1:200
$200K - $1M: Max 1:100
$1M+: Max 1:50

🔄 Hedging & Margin Requirements

When hedging (having both long and short positions on the same instrument), margin requirements may be reduced or waived depending on broker policy.

⚖️
Hedged Position Scenario
Long EUR/USD: 1 lot Margin: $2,000
Short EUR/USD: 1 lot Additional Margin: $0 (hedged)
Total Required Margin: $2,000 only

⚡ Correlation Risk Across Positions

Leverage risk compounds when holding multiple correlated positions. Example: Long EUR/USD + Long GBP/USD both lose if USD strengthens.

⚠️
Risk Management Strategy
  • Reduce leverage when holding multiple positions
  • Monitor total exposure across correlated pairs
  • Use stop-loss orders on every position
  • Never risk more than 1-2% per trade
  • Calculate total portfolio margin level regularly

Understanding Margin Call & Stop Out Levels

These are automatic safety mechanisms to protect you from losing more money than you have. EVERY trader must understand these levels before trading with leverage.

⚠️

Margin Call Level

80%

When your Margin Level drops to 80%, you receive a warning notification. You still have control, but action is needed.

📬 What Happens:
  • Email & platform notification sent
  • You can still open new positions
  • Recommended: Add funds or close losing positions
  • You have time to manage risk
💡 Formula:
Margin Level = (Equity ÷ Used Margin) × 100%
🛑

Stop Out Level

50%

When your Margin Level drops to 50%, the system automatically starts closing your positions to prevent further losses.

⚡ What Happens:
  • Automatic closure begins IMMEDIATELY
  • Most unprofitable position closed first
  • Process continues until margin level > 50%
  • You CANNOT open new positions
  • No manual control during stop out
⚠️ Critical: Stop Out is automatic and irreversible. Once triggered, you cannot prevent position closure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Real questions from traders, answered with specific examples

What leverage should I use as a beginner? ▼

Recommendation: Start with 1:10 to 1:30 maximum. As you gain experience and develop consistent risk management, you can gradually increase.

Why Low Leverage for Beginners?
• Gives you more breathing room for market volatility
• Reduces risk of Margin Call
• Helps you focus on learning strategy, not survival
• Professional traders often use 1:10-1:50 even after years of experience
Can I change my leverage after opening a position? ▼

No. Leverage is set when you open the position and cannot be changed while the position is active. You must close the position and re-open it with different leverage.

Example:
You open EUR/USD position with 1:100 leverage. Market moves against you, and you want to reduce risk by lowering leverage to 1:50.

Solution: Close the current position (at current loss), then re-open with 1:50 leverage. This will require more margin, so ensure you have sufficient balance.
How much margin do I need for 1 standard lot of EUR/USD? ▼

It depends on your leverage. Here's a quick reference:

1 Standard Lot EUR/USD = $100,000 position

• With 1:30 leverage → $3,333 margin required
• With 1:50 leverage → $2,000 margin required
• With 1:100 leverage → $1,000 margin required
• With 1:500 leverage → $200 margin required

Pro Tip: Always have at least 2-3x the required margin as free balance to avoid Margin Call.
What's the difference between Used Margin and Free Margin? ▼

Used Margin: Money "locked" to maintain open positions
Free Margin: Money available to open new positions or absorb losses

Example Calculation:
Account Balance: $10,000
Open Position Margin: $1,000
Current Profit/Loss: +$500

Equity = Balance + P/L = $10,000 + $500 = $10,500
Used Margin = $1,000
Free Margin = Equity - Used Margin = $10,500 - $1,000 = $9,500
Margin Level = (Equity ÷ Used Margin) × 100 = 1050%
Is high leverage illegal or dangerous? ▼

Legal: High leverage (1:500+) is legal in many jurisdictions, but regulated differently by region. EU/UK limit retail traders to 1:30, while other regions allow much higher.

Dangerous: High leverage is not inherently dangerous IF used with proper risk management. The danger comes from position sizing, not leverage itself.

Safe vs Risky Trading Example:

✓ SAFE: High leverage, small position
$10,000 account, 1:500 leverage, 0.01 lot EUR/USD
Risk: $10 per 100 pip move (0.1% of account)

✗ RISKY: Low leverage, large position
$10,000 account, 1:30 leverage, 1.0 lot EUR/USD
Risk: $1,000 per 100 pip move (10% of account)
