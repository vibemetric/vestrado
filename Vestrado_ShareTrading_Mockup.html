📈 Dividend Policy for Stock CFDs

When you trade stock CFDs, you don't own the underlying shares, but you still receive cash adjustments for dividends:

Long Positions (BUY)

✓ You RECEIVE dividend adjustments

If you hold a long CFD position when a stock goes ex-dividend, your account will be credited with the dividend amount (minus applicable tax).

Example:
• You buy 10 lots of AAPL CFD
• AAPL declares $0.24 dividend
• You receive: $0.24 × 100 × 10 = +$240 credit

Short Positions (SELL)

✗ You PAY dividend adjustments

If you hold a short CFD position when a stock goes ex-dividend, your account will be debited with the dividend amount.

Example:
• You short 10 lots of MSFT CFD
• MSFT declares $0.75 dividend
• You pay: $0.75 × 100 × 10 = -$750 charge

⚠️ Important Notes:

  • Ex-Dividend Date: Adjustments occur on the ex-dividend date, not the payment date
  • Tax Withholding: Some countries apply withholding tax (typically 15-30%)
  • Corporate Actions: Stock splits, rights issues handled similarly - see our Corporate Actions Policy

💰 Overnight Financing Fees (Swap Rates)

Important: Stock CFDs incur overnight financing fees if you hold positions past 5 PM ET (market close). This is a key difference from buying actual shares.

Financing Formula:

Overnight Fee = (Position Size × Stock Price × Financing Rate) / 360 days

📌 Typical Financing Rates:

  • Long Positions: LIBOR/SOFR + 2.5% annually (you PAY this fee)
  • Short Positions: LIBOR/SOFR - 2.5% annually (you may receive/pay depending on rate)
  • Daily Cost: Approximately 0.007% - 0.022% per day for most stocks

⚠️ Stock CFD-Specific Risks

Stock CFDs carry unique risks beyond general CFD trading:

  • Earnings Gap Risk: Stocks can gap significantly (5-20%+) after earnings announcements, potentially bypassing your stop-loss orders. Always reduce position sizes before major earnings.
  • Trading Halts: Individual stocks can be halted during trading hours due to pending news, circuit breakers, or regulatory actions. You cannot close positions during halts.
  • Overnight Financing Accumulation: Unlike forex, stock CFD financing costs compound quickly. A 30-day hold can cost 2-3% of position value. Not suitable for long-term holds.
  • Dividend-Related Volatility: Stocks typically drop by the dividend amount on ex-dividend date. If you're short, you pay the dividend PLUS the price drop impact.
  • Sector Correlation Risk: Tech stocks often move together. Diversifying across Apple, Google, Microsoft may not reduce risk as much as expected.
  • Single-Stock Concentration: Unlike indices, individual stocks can lose 50%+ of value permanently (bankruptcy, fraud, product failure). Use appropriate position sizing.
